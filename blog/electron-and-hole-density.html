Notes on the electron and hole density in solid state physics

16 November 2026

In solid state physics, we often introduce a harmonic oscillator (a fermionic oscillator in the case of fermions) for each mode of a dispersion relation. In other words, we write

\[H = \sum_{\mathbf{k}} E_{\mathbf{k}} a_{\mathbf{k}}^\dagger a_{\mathbf{k}}.\]

This allows us to compute the expected energy and the expected number of electrons or holes in a certain band. For the energy, we have

\[\langle E \rangle = \int dE\, E g(E) f(E),\]

where $g(E)$ is the density of states and $f(E)$ is the Fermi–Dirac distribution. This can be formalised by treating the Schrödinger equation as a field theory and canonically quantising it in the usual sense.

The Schrödinger Lagrangian is, with $\hbar = c = 1$,

\[\mathcal{L} = i\psi^\dagger \partial_t\psi - \frac{1}{2m}\nabla\psi^\dagger\cdot\nabla\psi - V(\mathbf{x})\psi^\dagger\psi.\]

The canonical momentum follows:

\[\pi = i\psi^\dagger.\]

For a system on the lattice, we have $V(\mathbf{x}) = V(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{R})$, where $\mathbf{R}\in\Lambda$, and $\Lambda$ is the set of all lattice vectors.

Let’s quantise the Schrödinger field theory in the Schrödinger picture. Since we are interested in electrons, we construct the Fock space through antisymmetric tensor products of the one-particle Hilbert space. That is, we quantise the system through the anticommutation relation

\[\{\psi_\alpha(\mathbf{x}),\psi_\beta^\dagger(\mathbf{y})\} = \delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta^3(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}),\]

where $\alpha,\beta$ are spinor components. By Bloch’s theorem, we can choose a complete basis of Bloch energy eigenfunctions and expand the field operator as

\[\psi(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{n\mathbf{k}s} u_{n\mathbf{k}s}(\mathbf{x})c_{n\mathbf{k}s},\]

where $n$ labels the potentially different energy bands1, and $s$ labels the spin of the particle2. Here $u_{n\mathbf{k}s}$ denotes the full Bloch wavefunction, including its plane-wave factor and spin state.

For a finite crystal with periodic boundary conditions, we take the Bloch wavefunctions to be normalised over the whole crystal:

\[\int d^3x\, u_{n\mathbf{k}s}^\dagger(\mathbf{x})u_{m\mathbf{q}r}(\mathbf{x}) = \delta_{nm}\delta_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{q}}\delta_{sr}.\]

We then find

\[c_{n\mathbf{k}s} = \int d^3x\, u_{n\mathbf{k}s}^\dagger(\mathbf{x})\psi(\mathbf{x}).\]

The equal-time anticommutation relation implies

\[\begin{aligned}\{c_{n\mathbf{k}s},c_{m\mathbf{q}r}^\dagger\} &= \int d^3x\,d^3y\,u_{n\mathbf{k}s}^\dagger(\mathbf{x})\{\psi(\mathbf{x}),\psi^\dagger(\mathbf{y})\}u_{m\mathbf{q}r}(\mathbf{y})\\ &= \delta_{nm}\delta_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{q}}\delta_{sr}.\end{aligned}\]

The Hamiltonian of the system is given by

\[\begin{aligned}H &= \int d^3x\,\psi^\dagger\left(-\frac{1}{2m}\nabla^2+V(\mathbf{x})\right)\psi\\ &= \sum_{n\mathbf{k}s}E_{n\mathbf{k}s}c_{n\mathbf{k}s}^\dagger c_{n\mathbf{k}s},\end{aligned}\]

as promised. The grand partition function is then

\[\begin{aligned}Z &= \operatorname{Tr}\,e^{-\beta(H-\mu N)}\\ &= \prod_{n\mathbf{k}s}\left[1+e^{-\beta(E_{n\mathbf{k}s}-\mu)}\right],\end{aligned}\]

which produces the Fermi–Dirac distribution for the occupation of each mode.

  1. In solid state physics, the energy spectrum of the Hamiltonian generally has several bands, which may be separated by gaps. ↩
  2. From the point of view of the scalar Schrödinger equation alone, the introduction of the $s$ label is additional input. However, the electron’s Schrödinger equation is a low-energy approximation of the Dirac equation, which is a theory for spinors. In the noninteracting approximation used here, spin-dependent terms vanish when magnetic fields and relativistic corrections are neglected. The one-particle Hamiltonian is then $h = (\mathbf{p}^2/(2m)+V(\mathbf{x}))\otimes I_2$. ↩
