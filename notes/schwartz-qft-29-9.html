Problem 29.9: Chiral Rotations and the Theta Angle

Source: M. D. Schwartz, Quantum Field Theory and the Standard Model, Problem 29.9.

Available formatsHTMLPDF ↓

Show that when multiple generations are rotated, then the $\theta$ angle shifts by $\arg\det(R^\dagger L)$.

For $N$ generations, the chiral rotations are \[\psi_R^i\to R^{ij}\psi_R^j, \qquad \psi_L^i\to L^{ij}\psi_L^j,\tag{1.1}\] where $R,L\in U(N)$. We decompose \[U(N)=SU(N)\times U(1),\tag{1.2}\] and write \[R=e^{ir/N}\widetilde R, \qquad L=e^{i\ell/N}\widetilde L,\tag{1.3}\] where \[\widetilde R,\widetilde L\in SU(N), \qquad \det\widetilde R=\det\widetilde L=1.\tag{1.4}\]

Under the $SU(N)$ transformations, the Grassmann measures transform as \[\prod_i\mathcal D\psi_R^i \to (\det\widetilde R)^{-1} \prod_i\mathcal D\psi_R^i = \prod_i\mathcal D\psi_R^i\tag{1.5}\] and \[\prod_i\mathcal D\psi_L^i \to (\det\widetilde L)^{-1} \prod_i\mathcal D\psi_L^i = \prod_i\mathcal D\psi_L^i.\tag{1.6}\] The conjugate-field measures are also invariant. Therefore, the $SU(N)$ parts of $R$ and $L$ do not change the complete functional measure. Only the $U(1)$ parts can shift the $\theta$ angle.

The remaining $U(1)$ transformations are \[\psi_R\to e^{ir/N}\psi_R, \qquad \psi_L\to e^{i\ell/N}\psi_L.\tag{1.7}\] These can be written as a vector rotation and an axial rotation: \[\psi\to e^{i\alpha_V}e^{i\alpha_A\gamma^5}\psi,\tag{1.8}\] where \[\alpha_V=\frac{r+\ell}{2N}, \qquad \alpha_A=\frac{r-\ell}{2N}.\tag{1.9}\] The vector rotation leaves the functional measure invariant. An axial rotation by $\alpha_A$ shifts the $\theta$ angle by $-2\alpha_A$ for each generation. Since there are $N$ generations, \[\Delta\theta=-2N\alpha_A =-2N\left(\frac{r-\ell}{2N}\right) =\ell-r.\tag{1.10}\]

Finally, \[\begin{aligned} \det(R^\dagger L) &=(\det R)^*\det L\\ &=e^{-ir}e^{i\ell}\\ &=e^{i(\ell-r)}. \end{aligned}\tag{1.11}\] Therefore, \[\arg\det(R^\dagger L)=\ell-r=\Delta\theta,\tag{1.12}\] and hence \[\boxed{ \theta\to\theta+\arg\det(R^\dagger L) }.\tag{1.13}\]
