Problem 29.8: Integrating Out Right-Handed Neutrinos

Source: M. D. Schwartz, Quantum Field Theory and the Standard Model, Problem 29.8.

Available formatsHTMLPDF ↓

Show that when you integrate out the right-handed neutrinos in Eq. (29.63), a dimension-5 operator like that in Eq. (29.65) results. What is the exact relationship between $M_{ij}$ and $\widetilde M_{ij}$?

The relevant Lagrangian for the right-handed neutrino is \[\mathcal{L} =i\bar\nu^i_R(\slashed{\partial}-ig'Y_\nu \slashed{B})\nu_R^i - Y_{ij}^\nu\bar L^i\tilde{H}\nu_R^j - iM_{ij}(\nu_R^i)^c\nu_R^j,\tag{1.1}\] where the hypercharge $Y_\nu$ vanishes. The equation of motion is given by \[-i(\partial_\mu \nu_R^{i\dagger})\sigma^\mu - Y_{ji}^{\nu}(\bar L^j \tilde H) -2iM_{ji}(\nu_R^{j})^T\sigma_2 = 0.\tag{1.2}\] At low energy compared to $M_{ij}$, the kinetic term of the right-handed neutrino can be neglected, and we find \[-iM_{ij}(\nu_R^j)^T\sigma_2 = -\frac{1}{2}Y^\nu_{ji}(\bar L^j\tilde H), \quad M_{ij}\nu_R^j = -\frac{1}{2}i\sigma_2Y^\nu_{ij}(\bar L^j\tilde H)^T,\tag{1.3}\] where we have used $M_{ij} = M_{ji}$. Therefore, we find \[-iM_{ij}(\nu_R^i)^c\nu_R^j = \frac{i}{4}Y_{ij}^\nu M^{-1}_{jk}Y_{kl}^\nu(\bar L^i\tilde H) \sigma_2(\bar L^l\tilde H)^T,\tag{1.4}\] which gives \[\boxed{ \widetilde M_{il} = - \frac{i}{4}Y_{ij}^\nu M^{-1}_{jk}Y_{kl}^\nu. }\tag{1.5}\]
