Problem 29.7: Neutrino Oscillations

Source: M. D. Schwartz, Quantum Field Theory and the Standard Model, Problem 29.7.

Available formatsHTMLPDF ↓

Neutrino oscillations.

  1. Neutrinos are produced in the Sun predominantly through the reaction $p+p+e^-\to d+\nu_e$. What is the momentum of the neutrinos produced this way?
  2. Consider a two-neutrino flavour system. The mass eigenstates evolve in time as \[\begin{aligned}|\nu_1\rangle &=e^{-iE_1t}\bigl(\cos\theta\,|\nu_e\rangle +\sin\theta\,|\nu_\mu\rangle\bigr),\\ |\nu_2\rangle &=e^{-iE_2t}\bigl(-\sin\theta\,|\nu_e\rangle +\cos\theta\,|\nu_\mu\rangle\bigr),\end{aligned}\tag{1.1}\] where $\theta$ is the mixing angle. Show that the probability of finding a solar neutrino as an electron neutrino after a time $T$ is given by \[P=1-\sin^2(2\theta)\sin^2\!\left(\frac{(E_2-E_1)T}{2}\right). \tag{1.2}\]
  3. Take the high-energy limit $E\gg m_\nu$ to show that the probability of finding a solar neutrino with energy $E$ as an electron neutrino at a distance $L$ is given by \[P=1-\sin^2(2\theta)\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\Delta m^2L}{4E}\right).\tag{1.3}\]
  4. How far should you put your detector from a reactor producing $\sim4\,\mathrm{MeV}$ neutrinos assuming $\Delta m^2=7.5\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{eV}^2$ to see the largest effect?

Neglecting the thermal kinetic energy of the particles, the four-momentum before reaction is given by $k^\mu = (2m_p + m_e = s, 0 )$. By four-momentum conservation $p_{\nu_e} + p_d = k$, we have \[p_d^2 = m_d^2 = (k-p_{\nu_e})^2 = s+ m_{\nu_e}^2 -2\sqrt{s}E_{\nu_e} \implies E_{\nu_e} = \frac{s+m_{\nu_e}^2 -m_d^2}{s\sqrt{s}} = \sqrt{m_d^2 +|\mathbf{p}_{\nu_e}|^2}.\tag{1.4}\] Therefore, we find \[|\mathbf{p}_{\nu_e}| \approx 1.44 ~\mathrm{GeV}.\tag{1.5}\]

The solar neutrino is produced as an electron neutrino. That is, at $t=0$, \[\ket{\nu;t=0} = \ket{\nu_e} = \cos\theta\ket{\nu_1} - \sin\theta \ket{\nu_2}.\tag{1.6}\] Carrying out a standard QM calculation, it is easy to obtain (1.2).

In the high-energy limit, \[E_1 \approx |\mathbf{p}|\left(1-\frac{m_1^2}{2|\mathbf{p}|^2}\right) \implies -\Delta E \approx \frac{2\Delta m^2}{2|\mathbf{p}|} \approx \frac{2\Delta m^2}{2E}.\tag{1.7}\] In natural units, $T \approx L$, and hence, we find the desired equation.

The maxima occur at \[L_n = \frac{2\pi E}{\Delta m^2}(2n+1), \quad L_0 \approx 66 ~\mathrm{km}.\tag{1.8}\]
