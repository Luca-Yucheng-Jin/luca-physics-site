Problem 29.3: Higgs Decays

Source: M. D. Schwartz, Quantum Field Theory and the Standard Model, Problem 29.3.

Available formatsHTMLPDF ↓

Higgs decays.

  1. Calculate the rate for $H\to b\bar b$ in the Standard Model.
  2. Calculate the rate for $H\to gg$ in the Standard Model. The dominant contribution to this comes from a triangle loop diagram involving top quarks.
  3. Calculate the rate for $H\to\gamma\gamma$ in the Standard Model. Include contributions both from top loops and from loops of $W$ bosons.
  4. Plot the branching ratios for $H\to b\bar b$, $H\to gg$, and $H\to\gamma\gamma$ as a function of Higgs mass.

Before symmetry breaking, the fermions couple to the Higgs field as \[-Y_{ij}^d\bar Q^iHd_R^j - Y_{ij}^u\bar Q^i \tilde{H} u_R^j + h.c.\tag{1.1}\] Focusing on the bottom quark, we find \[-Y_{33}^d \begin{pmatrix} \bar t_L& \bar b_L \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} H^+\\ H^0 \end{pmatrix}b_R + h.c.\tag{1.2}\] Expanding the Higgs around the vacuum as usual, we find the bottom quark couples to the Higgs field $h(x)$ through \[-\frac{y}{\sqrt{2}}\bar b_L h b_R - \frac{y^*}{\sqrt{2}}\bar b_R h b_L = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\operatorname{Re}y )h\bar b b -\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}} (\operatorname{Im}y)h \bar b \gamma^5 b,\tag{1.3}\] where we defined $y \equiv Y_{33}^d$ and we have assumed $y$ to be complex for generality. Therefore, at tree-level the scattering amplitude is given by \[i\mathcal{M}_b = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}i(\operatorname{Re}y )[\bar u(p_2)v(p_3)] + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\operatorname{Im}y)[\bar u(p_2)\gamma^5v(p_3)].\tag{1.4}\] Then, we find \[\begin{aligned} \sum_\mathrm{spin/colour}|\mathcal{M}_b|^2 &= \frac{3}{2}\sum_\mathrm{spin}\bigg[-(\operatorname{Re}y )[\bar u(p_2)v(p_3)] -i (\operatorname{Im}y)[\bar u(p_2)\gamma^5v(p_3)]\bigg]\\ &\times\bigg[-(\operatorname{Re}y )[\bar v(p_3)u(p_2)] -i (\operatorname{Im}y)[\bar v(p_3)\gamma^5u(p_2)]\bigg]\\ &=\frac{3}{2}[(\operatorname{Re}y )^2 + (\operatorname{Im}y )^2]\Tr((\slashed{p}_2+m_b)(\slashed{p}_3 -m_b))\\ &=6|y|^2(p_2\cdot p_3 - m_b^2). \end{aligned}\tag{1.5}\] In the mass basis, $y$ can be chosen real. To compute the decay rate in the centre-of-mass frame, we use \[\Gamma = \frac{1}{32\pi^2m_h^2} |\mathbf p| \int d\Omega\, |\mathcal M_b|^2. \tag{1.6}\] Using \[s=m_h^2, \qquad |\mathbf p| = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{m_h^2-4m_b^2}, \qquad p_2\cdot p_3 = \frac{m_h^2-2m_b^2}{2},\tag{1.7}\] we find \[\boxed{ \Gamma(H\to b\bar b) = \frac{3y^2}{16\pi m_h^2} (m_h^2-4m_b^2)^{3/2} = \frac{3m_b^2m_h}{8\pi v^2} \left( 1-\frac{4m_b^2}{m_h^2} \right)^{3/2}. }\tag{1.8}\] Using \[m_h=125.20~\mathrm{GeV}, \qquad m_b=4.183~\mathrm{GeV}, \qquad v=246.22~\mathrm{GeV},\tag{1.9}\] we obtain \[\boxed{ \Gamma(H\to b\bar b) \simeq 4.28~\mathrm{MeV}. }\tag{1.10}\]

There is no tree-level contribution to $H \to gg$. The lowest-order contribution comes from the quark triangle diagram

$\displaystyle \quad +\quad (u-\text{channel}).$(1.11)

For a particular quark flavour $q$, the scattering amplitude is \[\begin{aligned} i\mathcal M_q^{ab} &= -\left(-i\frac{y_q}{\sqrt{2}}\right) (ig_s)^2 \int\frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d} \\ &\quad\times \Tr\left[ \frac{i(\slashed{k}+m_q)} {k^2-m_q^2} \gamma^\mu T^a \frac{i(\slashed{k}-\slashed{p}_2+m_q)} {(k-p_2)^2-m_q^2} \gamma^\nu T^b \frac{i(\slashed{k}-\slashed{p}_1+m_q)} {(k-p_1)^2-m_q^2} \right] \epsilon_\mu^*(p_2)\epsilon_\nu^*(p_3) \\ &\quad+ \left( p_2,\mu,a\leftrightarrow p_3,\nu,b \right)\\ &=i\frac{y_qg_s^2}{\sqrt{2}}T_F\delta^{ab} \epsilon_\mu^*(p_2)\epsilon_\nu^*(p_3)\,8m_q \left[ F_q^{\mu\nu}(p_1,p_2) +F_q^{\nu\mu}(p_1,p_3) \right], \end{aligned}\tag{1.12}\] where $ y_q=\frac{\sqrt2 m_q}{v} =2\sqrt{\lambda} \frac{m_q}{m_h} $, and $F_q^{\mu\nu}$ is defined to include the factor of $i$ below, so that it is real for the top-quark loop. Since $y_q\propto m_q$ and $m_b,m_c,m_s,m_d,m_u\ll m_h$, the dominant contribution comes from the top-quark loop. The complete amplitude is obtained by summing over quark flavours. Now, we evaluate the loop integral. The trace can be simplified as \[\begin{aligned} &\Tr\left[ \frac{i(\slashed{k}+m_q)} {k^2-m_q^2} \gamma^\mu \frac{i(\slashed{k}-\slashed{p}_2+m_q)} {(k-p_2)^2-m_q^2} \gamma^\nu \frac{i(\slashed{k}-\slashed{p}_1+m_q)} {(k-p_1)^2-m_q^2} \right] \\[2mm] &= -\frac{4im_q}{(k^2-m_q^2)[(k-p_2)^2-m_q^2][(k-p_1)^2-m_q^2]} \Big[ k^\mu(k-p_2)^\nu +k^\nu(k-p_2)^\mu -k\cdot(k-p_2)\eta^{\mu\nu} \\ &\qquad +k^\mu(k-p_1)^\nu -k^\nu(k-p_1)^\mu +k\cdot(k-p_1)\eta^{\mu\nu} +(k-p_2)^\mu(k-p_1)^\nu +(k-p_1)^\mu(k-p_2)^\nu \\ &\qquad -(k-p_2)\cdot(k-p_1)\eta^{\mu\nu} +m_q^2\eta^{\mu\nu} \Big]. \end{aligned}\tag{1.13}\] Let \[A=k^2-m_q^2,\quad B=(k-p_2)^2-m_q^2,\quad C=(k-p_1)^2-m_q^2.\tag{1.14}\] Introducing Feynman parameter, \[\frac{1}{ABC} = 2\int_0^1dx\int_0^{1-x}dy\, \frac{1}{[xA+yB+zC]^3}, \qquad z=1-x-y,\tag{1.15}\] the denominator becomes \[\begin{aligned} xA+yB+zC &= k^2-2k\cdot(yp_2+zp_1) +yp_2^2+zp_1^2-m_q^2 \\ &= (k-yp_2-zp_1)^2-\Delta, \end{aligned}\tag{1.16}\] where \[\Delta = m_q^2-y(1-y)p_2^2-z(1-z)p_1^2 +2yz\,p_1\cdot p_2.\tag{1.17}\] Shifting the loop momentum, $\ell = k - yp_2-zp_1\equiv k - P$, the numerator becomes \[\begin{aligned} \mathcal N^{\mu\nu} ={}& 4\ell^\mu\ell^\nu-\ell^2\eta^{\mu\nu} +4P^\mu P^\nu -2P^\mu(p_1+p_2)^\nu -2P^\nu p_2^\mu \\ &+p_1^\mu p_2^\nu+p_2^\mu p_1^\nu +\left( m_q^2-P^2+2P\cdot p_2-p_1\cdot p_2 \right)\eta^{\mu\nu} \\ &+4\ell^\mu P^\nu+4P^\mu\ell^\nu -2\ell^\mu(p_1+p_2)^\nu -2\ell^\nu p_2^\mu +2\ell\cdot(p_2-P)\eta^{\mu\nu}. \end{aligned}\tag{1.18}\] The loop integral becomes \[\begin{aligned} F^{\mu\nu}_q(p_1,p_2) &= i\int_0^1dx\int_0^{1-x}dy \int\frac{d^d\ell}{(2\pi)^d} \frac{\mathcal N^{\mu\nu}(\ell,P)} {(\ell^2-\Delta)^3}. \end{aligned}\tag{1.19}\] The terms with an odd number of $\ell$ in the numerator vanish, and $\ell^\mu \ell^\nu \to \frac{1}{d}\eta^{\mu\nu}\ell^2$, which gives \[\begin{aligned} \mathcal N^{\mu\nu} \longrightarrow{}& \left(\frac{4}{d}-1\right)\ell^2\eta^{\mu\nu} +4P^\mu P^\nu -2P^\mu(p_1+p_2)^\nu -2P^\nu p_2^\mu \\ &+p_1^\mu p_2^\nu+p_2^\mu p_1^\nu +\left( m_q^2-P^2+2P\cdot p_2-p_1\cdot p_2 \right)\eta^{\mu\nu}. \end{aligned}\tag{1.20}\] Then, using dimensional regularisation expanding around $d=4-\epsilon$, we find \[\begin{aligned} F_q^{\mu\nu}(p_1,p_2) = \frac{-1}{32\pi^2} \int_0^1dx\int_0^{1-x}dy\, \frac{1}{m_q^2-xz\,m_h^2} \Bigg[ &m_h^2\left(\frac12-2xz\right)\eta^{\mu\nu} \\ &-2z(2z-1)p_1^\mu p_1^\nu +4y(1-y)p_2^\mu p_2^\nu \\ &-\left(4yz-2z+1\right)p_1^\mu p_2^\nu \\ &-\left(4yz-2y-2z+1\right)p_2^\mu p_1^\nu \Bigg]. \end{aligned}\tag{1.21}\]

Contracting with the on-shell gluon polarisations, it follows that \[\begin{aligned} &\epsilon_\mu^*(p_2)\epsilon_\nu^*(p_3) p_1^\mu p_1^\nu = \epsilon_\mu^*(p_2)\epsilon_\nu^*(p_3) p_1^\mu p_2^\nu \\ &\qquad= \bigl[\epsilon^*(p_2)\cdot p_3\bigr] \bigl[\epsilon^*(p_3)\cdot p_2\bigr], \end{aligned}\tag{1.22}\] while all terms proportional to $p_2^\mu$ vanish. Therefore, \[\begin{aligned} &\epsilon_\mu^*(p_2)\epsilon_\nu^*(p_3) F_t^{\mu\nu}(p_1,p_2) \\ &\quad= -\frac{1}{32\pi^2} \int_0^1dx\int_0^{1-x}dy\, \frac{1-4xz}{m_t^2-xz\,m_h^2} \\ &\qquad\quad\times \left[ \frac{m_h^2}{2}\, \epsilon^*(p_2)\cdot\epsilon^*(p_3) - \bigl(\epsilon^*(p_2)\cdot p_3\bigr) \bigl(\epsilon^*(p_3)\cdot p_2\bigr) \right]. \end{aligned}\tag{1.23}\] The crossed orientation gives the same result after relabelling the Feynman parameters. Adding the two orientations gives \[\begin{aligned} &\epsilon_\mu^*(p_2)\epsilon_\nu^*(p_3) \left[ F_t^{\mu\nu}(p_1,p_2)+F_t^{\nu\mu}(p_1,p_3) \right] \\ &\quad= -\frac{1}{16\pi^2} \left[ \frac{m_h^2}{2}\, \epsilon^*(p_2)\cdot\epsilon^*(p_3) - \bigl(\epsilon^*(p_2)\cdot p_3\bigr) \bigl(\epsilon^*(p_3)\cdot p_2\bigr) \right] \\ &\qquad\quad\times \int_0^1dx\int_0^{1-x}dy\, \frac{1-4x(1-x-y)} {m_t^2-m_h^2x(1-x-y)}. \end{aligned}\tag{1.24}\] Evaluating the integral gives \[\begin{aligned} &\int_0^1dx\int_0^{1-x}dy\, \frac{1-4x(1-x-y)} {m_t^2-m_h^2x(1-x-y)} \\ &\qquad= \frac{2}{m_h^2} \left[ 1+\left(1-\frac{4m_t^2}{m_h^2}\right) \arcsin^2\left(\frac{m_h}{2m_t}\right) \right]. \end{aligned}\tag{1.25}\] Thus, the top-loop amplitude becomes \[\begin{aligned} i\mathcal M_t^{ab} ={}& -i\frac{y_tg_s^2m_tT_F}{2\sqrt{2}\pi^2}\, \delta^{ab} \left[ 1+\left(1-\frac{4m_t^2}{m_h^2}\right) \arcsin^2\left(\frac{m_h}{2m_t}\right) \right] \\ &\times \epsilon_\mu^*(p_2)\epsilon_\nu^*(p_3) \left[ \eta^{\mu\nu} -\frac{2}{m_h^2}p_3^\mu p_2^\nu \right]. \end{aligned}\tag{1.26}\] Using \[\sum_{\mathrm{pols.}} \epsilon_\mu^*(p)\epsilon_\nu(p) =-\eta_{\mu\nu}, \qquad \sum_{a,b}\delta^{ab}\delta^{ab}=N_c^2-1=8, \qquad T_F=\frac12,\tag{1.27}\] and \[\left[ \eta^{\mu\nu}-\frac{2}{m_h^2}p_3^\mu p_2^\nu \right] \left[ \eta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{2}{m_h^2}p_{3\mu}p_{2\nu} \right] =2,\tag{1.28}\] we finally obtain \[\begin{aligned} \sum_{\mathrm{pols./colours}}|\mathcal M_t|^2 ={}& \frac{m_t^2y_t^2g_s^4}{2\pi^4} \left[ 1+\left(1-\frac{4m_t^2}{m_h^2}\right) \arcsin^2\left(\frac{m_h}{2m_t}\right) \right]^2. \end{aligned}\tag{1.29}\] Here we have neglected the contributions from the remaining quarks.

Plugging into (1.6), we find \[\begin{aligned} \boxed{ \Gamma(H \to gg) = \frac{\lambda g_s^4 m_t^4}{16\pi^5m_h^3}\left[ 1+\left(1-\frac{4m_t^2}{m_h^2}\right) \arcsin^2\left(\frac{m_h}{2m_t}\right) \right]^2,} \end{aligned}\tag{1.30}\] where a factor of $1/2$ is added since the outgoing gluon states are identical. This gives a numerical value of \[\boxed{ \Gamma(H\to gg) \simeq 1.97\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{GeV} =0.197\ \mathrm{MeV}. }\tag{1.31}\]

Now we turn to the computation of $H \to \gamma\gamma$. Similarly to the gluon case, there is no tree-level contribution to the process. The dominant quark-loop contribution comes from the top-quark loop, just as for the gluon case. On top of that, there is also a contribution from a $W$-boson loop. Therefore, our job is to compute the following diagrams

$\displaystyle \quad+\quad \left(p_2,\mu\leftrightarrow p_3,\nu\right) \quad=\quad i\mathcal M_t^\gamma.$(1.32)
$\displaystyle \quad+\quad \left(p_2,\mu\leftrightarrow p_3,\nu\right) \quad=\quad i\mathcal M_W,$(1.33)
$\displaystyle \quad = \quad i\mathcal M_{W,4}^{\mu\nu}.$(1.34)

The relevant interactions are \[\begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}_\mathrm{int} =&-ie[F^{\mu\nu}W^+_\mu W^-_\nu-(\partial_\mu W^+_\nu - \partial_\nu W^+_\mu)A^\mu (W^-)^\nu+(\partial_\mu W^-_\nu - \partial_\nu W^-_\mu)A^\mu (W^+)^\nu]\\ &+ 2\frac{h}{v}m_W^2W_\mu^+ W_\nu ^- +e^2(A^\mu W_\mu^+A^\nu W_\nu^- - A_\mu^2W_\nu^+(W^-)^\nu)- \frac{y_t}{\sqrt{2}}h\bar t t + \frac{2e}{3}A_\mu \bar t \gamma^\mu t, \end{aligned}\tag{1.35}\] where the first three interactions are given by the diagrams

$\displaystyle \quad = \quad i\frac{e\,m_W}{\sin\theta_w}\eta^{\mu\nu},$(1.36)
$\displaystyle \quad = \quad -ie [t] \Big[ \eta^{\mu\nu}(p_1-p_2)^\lambda +\eta^{\nu\lambda}(p_2-p_3)^\mu$$\displaystyle +\eta^{\lambda\mu}(p_3-p_1)^\nu \Big],$(1.37)
$\displaystyle \quad = \quad ie^2 [t] \Big[ \eta^{\alpha\mu}\eta^{\beta\nu} +\eta^{\alpha\nu}\eta^{\beta\mu} -2\eta^{\alpha\beta}\eta^{\mu\nu} \Big].$(1.38)

and the last two are Yukawa and QED interactions. Note that $i\mathcal{M}_t^\gamma$ is almost identical to $i\mathcal{M}_t$, but now the colour trace gives $N_c=3$ and $g_s \to 2e/3$. Therefore, we can read off from (1.26), \[\begin{aligned} i\mathcal M_t^{\gamma} ={}& -i\frac{2y_te^2m_t}{3\sqrt{2}\pi^2}\, \left[ 1+\left(1-\frac{4m_t^2}{m_h^2}\right) \arcsin^2\left(\frac{m_h}{2m_t}\right) \right] \\ &\times \epsilon_\mu^*(p_2)\epsilon_\nu^*(p_3) \left[ \eta^{\mu\nu} -\frac{2}{m_h^2}p_3^\mu p_2^\nu \right]. \end{aligned}\tag{1.39}\] Although rather tedious, we can also read off the $W$ bosons loop diagram from the Feynman rules (or one can easily obtain the expression using the power of AI). In unitary gauge, the $W$-boson propagator is \[D_W^{\alpha\beta}(q) = \frac{-iP^{\alpha\beta}(q)} {q^2-m_W^2}, \qquad P^{\alpha\beta}(q) \equiv \eta^{\alpha\beta} -\frac{q^\alpha q^\beta}{m_W^2}.\tag{1.40}\] It is useful to define the momentum-dependent part of the $\gamma W^+W^-$ vertex by \[\begin{aligned} \Gamma^{\lambda\alpha\beta}(q,r,s) ={}& \eta^{\alpha\beta}(r-s)^\lambda +\eta^{\beta\lambda}(s-q)^\alpha +\eta^{\lambda\alpha}(q-r)^\beta, \end{aligned}\tag{1.41}\] so that the vertex is $-ie\Gamma^{\lambda\alpha\beta}(q,r,s)$.

The triangle diagram and its crossed photon ordering together give \[\begin{aligned} i\mathcal M_{W} ={}& \frac{e^3m_W}{\sin\theta_w} \epsilon_\mu^*(p_2)\epsilon_\nu^*(p_3) \int\frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d} \Bigg[ \frac{\mathcal N^{\mu\nu}(k;p_2,p_3)} {(k^2-m_W^2) [(k-p_2)^2-m_W^2] [(k-p_1)^2-m_W^2]} \\ & + \frac{\mathcal N^{\nu\mu}(k;p_3,p_2)} {(k^2-m_W^2) [(k-p_3)^2-m_W^2] [(k-p_1)^2-m_W^2]} \Bigg], \end{aligned}\tag{1.42}\] where \[\begin{aligned} \mathcal N^{\mu\nu}(k;p_2,p_3) ={}& \eta^{\alpha\rho} P_{\alpha\beta}(k) \Gamma^{\mu\beta\gamma} (-p_2,k,p_2-k) \\ &\times P_{\gamma\delta}(k-p_2) \Gamma^{\nu\delta\sigma} (-p_3,k-p_2,p_1-k) P_{\sigma\rho}(k-p_1). \end{aligned}\tag{1.43}\] The quartic $\gamma\gamma W^+W^-$ bubble diagram must also be included. Define \[Q^{\mu\nu\beta\sigma} = \eta^{\mu\beta}\eta^{\nu\sigma} +\eta^{\mu\sigma}\eta^{\nu\beta} -2\eta^{\mu\nu}\eta^{\beta\sigma},\tag{1.44}\] so that the quartic vertex is $ie^2Q^{\mu\nu\beta\sigma}$. Its contribution is \[\begin{aligned} i\mathcal M_{W,4} ={}& \frac{e^3m_W}{\sin\theta_w} \epsilon_\mu^*(p_2)\epsilon_\nu^*(p_3) \int\frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d} \frac{ \eta^{\alpha\rho} P_{\alpha\beta}(k) Q^{\mu\nu\beta\sigma} P_{\sigma\rho}(k-p_1) }{ (k^2-m_W^2)[(k-p_1)^2-m_W^2] }. \end{aligned}\tag{1.45}\]

Now, we shall evaluate the two diagrams. Introduce Feynman parameters, \[\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{D_0D_2D_1} &= 2\int_0^1dx\int_0^{1-x}dy\, \frac{1}{[xD_0+yD_2+zD_1]^3}, \\ \frac{1}{D_0D_3D_1} &= 2\int_0^1dx\int_0^{1-x}dy\, \frac{1}{[xD_0+yD_3+zD_1]^3}, \qquad z=1-x-y, \end{aligned}\tag{1.46}\] where \[\begin{aligned} D_0&=k^2-m_W^2, & D_1&=(k-p_1)^2-m_W^2, \\ D_2&=(k-p_2)^2-m_W^2, & D_3&=(k-p_3)^2-m_W^2. \end{aligned}\tag{1.47}\] For the first and crossed orientations, respectively, make the shifts \[\begin{aligned} \ell&=k-yp_2-zp_1, & \ell&=k-yp_3-zp_1. \end{aligned}\tag{1.48}\] Using \[p_1=p_2+p_3, \qquad p_1^2=m_h^2, \qquad p_2^2=p_3^2=0,\tag{1.49}\] both denominators reduce to \[xD_0+yD_{2,3}+zD_1 = \ell^2-\Delta, \qquad \Delta=m_W^2-xz\,m_h^2.\tag{1.50}\] Therefore, the two triangle orientations together become \[\begin{aligned} i\mathcal M_{W} ={}& 2\frac{e^3m_W}{\sin\theta_w} \epsilon_\mu^*(p_2)\epsilon_\nu^*(p_3) \int_0^1dx\int_0^{1-x}dy \int\frac{d^d\ell}{(2\pi)^d} \\ &\times \frac{ \mathcal N^{\mu\nu} \bigl(\ell+yp_2+zp_1;p_2,p_3\bigr) + \mathcal N^{\nu\mu} \bigl(\ell+yp_3+zp_1;p_3,p_2\bigr) }{ (\ell^2-\Delta)^3 }. \end{aligned}\tag{1.51}\] For convenience, define the combined numerator \[\begin{aligned} \widetilde{\mathcal N}^{\mu\nu}(\ell;x,y) \equiv{}& \mathcal N^{\mu\nu} \bigl(\ell+yp_2+zp_1;p_2,p_3\bigr) \\ &+ \mathcal N^{\nu\mu} \bigl(\ell+yp_3+zp_1;p_3,p_2\bigr). \end{aligned}\tag{1.52}\] Thus, \[\begin{aligned} i\mathcal M_{W} ={}& 2\frac{e^3m_W}{\sin\theta_w} \epsilon_\mu^*(p_2)\epsilon_\nu^*(p_3) \int_0^1dx\int_0^{1-x}dy \int\frac{d^d\ell}{(2\pi)^d} \frac{ \widetilde{\mathcal N}^{\mu\nu}(\ell;x,y) }{ (\ell^2-\Delta)^3 }. \end{aligned}\tag{1.53}\] To ensure convergence we should include the four-point diagram before carrying out the loop integral. The terms with odd $l$ in the numerator vanishes and $l^\mu l^\nu \to \frac{1}{d}\eta^{\mu\nu}l^2$, the $l^4$ term in $i\mathcal{M}_{W,4}$ becomes \[\begin{aligned} \ell^\mu\ell^\nu\ell^\rho\ell^\sigma \longrightarrow \frac{\ell^4}{d(d+2)} \Big( &\eta^{\mu\nu}\eta^{\rho\sigma} +\eta^{\mu\rho}\eta^{\nu\sigma} +\eta^{\mu\sigma}\eta^{\nu\rho} \Big). \end{aligned}\tag{1.54}\] After some long and cumbersome algebra, one would find \[\begin{aligned} i\mathcal M_W+i\mathcal M_{W,4} ={}& i\frac{e^3} {16\pi^2m_W\sin\theta_w} \epsilon_\mu^*(p_2)\epsilon_\nu^*(p_3) \left[ (p_2\cdot p_3)\eta^{\mu\nu} -p_3^\mu p_2^\nu \right] \\ &\times \left[ 2+ 12m_W^2 \int_0^1dx\int_0^{1-x}dy\, \frac{1-2xz}{m_W^2-m_h^2xz} \right]. \end{aligned}\tag{1.55}\] The integral can be evaluated, which gives \[\begin{aligned} \int_0^1dx\int_0^{1-x}dy\, \frac{1-2xz}{m_W^2-m_h^2xz} = \frac{1}{m_h^2} \left[ 1+\left(2-\frac{4m_W^2}{m_h^2}\right)\arcsin^2\left(\frac{m_h}{2m_W}\right) \right]. \end{aligned}\tag{1.56}\] Finally, we end up with \[\begin{aligned} i\mathcal M_W + i\mathcal M_{W,4} ={}& i\frac{e^3} {16\pi^2m_W\sin\theta_w} \Bigg[ 2+\frac{12m_W^2}{m_h^2} +\frac{24m_W^2}{m_h^2} \left( 1-\frac{2m_W^2}{m_h^2} \right) \arcsin^2\left(\frac{m_h}{2m_W}\right) \Bigg] \\ &\times \epsilon_\mu^*(p_2)\epsilon_\nu^*(p_3) \left[ (p_2\cdot p_3)\eta^{\mu\nu} -p_3^\mu p_2^\nu \right]. \end{aligned}\tag{1.57}\]

To combine the top and $W$ contributions, define \[\begin{aligned} \mathcal F_t ={}& \frac{32m_t^2}{3m_h^2} \left[ 1+\left(1-\frac{4m_t^2}{m_h^2}\right) \arcsin^2\left(\frac{m_h}{2m_t}\right) \right], \\ \mathcal F_W ={}& 2+\frac{12m_W^2}{m_h^2} +\frac{24m_W^2}{m_h^2} \left(1-\frac{2m_W^2}{m_h^2}\right) \arcsin^2\left(\frac{m_h}{2m_W}\right). \end{aligned}\tag{1.58}\] Using $y_t=\sqrt{2}m_t/v$ and $m_W=e v/(2\sin\theta_w)$, the total amplitude is \[\begin{aligned} i\mathcal M_{\gamma\gamma} ={}& i\frac{e^3}{16\pi^2m_W\sin\theta_w} (\mathcal F_W-\mathcal F_t) \epsilon_\mu^*(p_2)\epsilon_\nu^*(p_3) \left[ (p_2\cdot p_3)\eta^{\mu\nu} -p_3^\mu p_2^\nu \right]. \end{aligned}\tag{1.59}\] Thus, using $p_2\cdot p_3=m_h^2/2$, the polarization sum gives \[\begin{aligned} \sum_{\mathrm{pols.}}|\mathcal M_{\gamma\gamma}|^2 &= \frac{e^6}{256\pi^4m_W^2\sin^2\theta_w} (\mathcal F_W-\mathcal F_t)^2 \left[ (p_2\cdot p_3)\eta^{\mu\nu}-p_3^\mu p_2^\nu \right] \left[ (p_2\cdot p_3)\eta_{\mu\nu}-p_{3\mu}p_{2\nu} \right] \\ &= \frac{e^6m_h^4}{512\pi^4m_W^2\sin^2\theta_w} (\mathcal F_W-\mathcal F_t)^2. \end{aligned}\tag{1.60}\] The two photons are identical, so the two-body phase space includes a factor of $1/2!$. Therefore, \[\boxed{ \Gamma(H\to\gamma\gamma) = \frac{e^6m_h^3}{16384\pi^5m_W^2\sin^2\theta_w} (\mathcal F_W-\mathcal F_t)^2 = \frac{\alpha^2m_h^3}{256\pi^3v^2} (\mathcal F_W-\mathcal F_t)^2. }\tag{1.61}\] Using \[\begin{aligned} m_h&=125.20~\mathrm{GeV}, &m_t&=172.56~\mathrm{GeV}, &m_W&=80.377~\mathrm{GeV}, \\ v&=246.22~\mathrm{GeV}, &\alpha^{-1}&=137.036, \end{aligned}\tag{1.62}\] we find \[\mathcal F_t\simeq1.836, \qquad \mathcal F_W\simeq8.331,\tag{1.63}\] and hence \[\boxed{ \Gamma(H\to\gamma\gamma) \simeq9.16\times10^{-6}~\mathrm{GeV} =9.16~\mathrm{keV}. }\tag{1.64}\]
