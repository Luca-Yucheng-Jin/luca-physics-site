1 Computing Correlation Functions Using Generating Functionals

Work in Euclidean signature with the interacting scalar-field action \[S[\phi]=S_0[\phi]+\int d^4x\,V(\phi), \qquad V(\phi)=\frac{g}{4!}\phi^4,\tag{1.1}\] where $S_0[\phi]$ is the free Euclidean action.

  1. Introduce a source $J(x)$ and consider the free generating functional \[\mathcal Z_0[J] =\int\mathcal D\phi\, \exp\!\left[ -\frac{1}{\hbar} \left( S_0[\phi]-\int d^4x\,J(x)\phi(x) \right) \right].\tag{1.2}\] Evaluate this Gaussian functional integral. As a hint, first shift the integration variable by a function determined by $J$.

    In the Euclidean picture, we have \[S_0[\phi] = -\frac{1}{2}\phi (\partial^2 - m^2)\phi.\tag{1.3}\] To evaluate the integral, we make the shift of the integration variable, \[\bar \phi(x) = \phi(x) + \int d^dy \Delta(x-y)J(y) \equiv \phi_x + \Delta_{xy}J_y,\tag{1.4}\] where we have used the generalised summation convention and $(\partial^2 - m^2)\Delta(x-y) = \delta^d(x-y)$. Since $\mathcal{D}\phi = \mathcal{D}\bar \phi$, we have \[\begin{aligned} Z_0[J] &= \int \mathcal{D}\bar \phi\exp\left[-\frac{1}{\hbar}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\bar \phi_x(\partial^2-m^2)_{xy}\bar \phi_y + \frac{1}{2}J_x\Delta_{xy}J_y\right)\right] \\ &=\exp\left[-\frac{1}{\hbar}\int d^dx\int d^dy \frac{1}{2}J(x)\Delta(x-y)J(y)\right]Z_0[0]. \end{aligned}\tag{1.5}\]

  2. For an arbitrary polynomial interaction $V(\phi)$, establish the identity \[\begin{aligned}&\exp\!\left[ -\frac{1}{\hbar}\int d^4x\,V(\phi) +\frac{1}{\hbar}\int d^4x\,J(x)\phi(x) \right] \notag\\ &\qquad= \exp\!\left[ -\frac{1}{\hbar}\int d^4x\, V\!\left(\hbar\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}\right) \right] \exp\!\left[ \frac{1}{\hbar}\int d^4x\,J(x)\phi(x) \right].\end{aligned}\tag{1.6}\]

    \[\begin{aligned} &\exp\!\left[ -\frac{1}{\hbar}\int d^4x\,V(\phi) +\frac{1}{\hbar}\int d^4x\,J(x)\phi(x) \right]\\ &= \sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{1}{n!}\left(-\frac{1}{\hbar}\right)^n\left(\int d^dxV(\phi)\right)^n\exp\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}\int d^dx J(x) \phi(x)\right)\\ &=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{1}{n!}\left(-\frac{1}{\hbar}\right)^n\left(\int d^dx V\left(\hbar \frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}\right)\right)^n\exp\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}\int d^dx J(x) \phi(x)\right)\\ &=\exp\!\left[ -\frac{1}{\hbar}\int d^4x\, V\!\left(\hbar\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}\right) \right] \exp\!\left[ \frac{1}{\hbar}\int d^4x\,J(x)\phi(x) \right]. \end{aligned}\tag{1.7}\]

  3. For the interacting theory, define \[\mathcal Z[J] =\int\mathcal D\phi\, \exp\!\left[ -\frac{1}{\hbar} \left( S[\phi]-\int d^4x\,J(x)\phi(x) \right) \right].\tag{1.8}\] Use the preceding result to express $\mathcal Z[J]$ formally as a functional differential operator acting on $\mathcal Z_0[J]$.

    From b), we have \[Z[J] = \int \mathcal{D}\phi\exp\!\left[ -\frac{1}{\hbar}\int d^4x\, V\!\left(\hbar\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}\right) \right]\exp\!\left[ -\frac{1}{\hbar} \left( S_0[\phi]-\int d^4x\,J(x)\phi(x) \right) \right].\tag{1.9}\] Then, using the same field redefinition in a), we find \[Z[J] = \exp\!\left[ -\frac{1}{\hbar}\int d^4x\, V\!\left(\hbar\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}\right) \right]\exp\left[-\frac{1}{\hbar}\int d^dx\int d^dy \frac{1}{2}J(x)\Delta(x-y)J(y)\right]Z_0[0],\tag{1.10}\] or equivalently, \[Z[J] = \exp\!\left[ -\frac{1}{\hbar}\int d^4x\, V\!\left(\hbar\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}\right) \right]Z_0[J].\tag{1.11}\]

  4. The two-point Green function may be obtained from \[G(x_1,x_2) =\langle\phi(x_1)\phi(x_2)\rangle =\left. \frac{\hbar^2}{\mathcal Z[0]} \frac{\delta^2\mathcal Z[J]} {\delta J(x_1)\delta J(x_2)} \right|_{J=0}.\tag{1.12}\] Compute its perturbative expansion through order $g^2$ in $\phi^4$ theory. At each order, list every contribution without evaluating the position-space integrals, draw its Feynman diagram, and give the correct symmetry factor.

    Using the results from above, we have \[G(x_1,x_2) = \hbar^2\frac{\delta^2}{\delta_1 \delta_2}\exp\!\left[ -\frac{1}{\hbar}\int d^4x\, V\!\left(\hbar\frac{\delta}{\delta_x}\right) \right]\exp\left[-\frac{1}{\hbar} \frac{1}{2}J_x\Delta_{xy}J_y\right]\bigg|_{J=0}\frac{Z_0[0]}{Z[0]},\tag{1.13}\] where $Z[0]$ can be written as \[Z[0] = \exp\!\left[ -\frac{1}{\hbar}\int d^4x\, V\!\left(\hbar\frac{\delta}{\delta_x}\right) \right]\exp\left[-\frac{1}{\hbar} \frac{1}{2}J_x\Delta_{xy}J_y\right]\bigg|_{J=0}Z_0[0].\tag{1.14}\] Expanding the first exponential to order $g^2$, we have \[\begin{aligned} \exp\!\left[-\frac{1}{\hbar}\int d^4x\, V\!\left(\hbar\frac{\delta}{\delta_x}\right)\right] ={}&1-\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{g}{4!}\int d^dx\, \left(\hbar     Hence \[\begin{aligned} G_{12} &=\left[-\hbar\Delta_{12} +\frac{g}{8}\hbar^2\Delta_{12}\int d^dx\,\Delta_{xx}^2 +\frac{g}{2}\hbar^2\int d^dx\, \Delta_{xx}\Delta_{1x}\Delta_{2x} +\mathcal O(g^2)\right]\\ &\quad\times \left[1+\frac{g}{8}\hbar\int d^dy\,\Delta_{yy}^2 +\mathcal O(g^2)\right]\\ &=-\hbar\Delta_{12} +\frac{g}{2}\hbar^2\int d^dx\, \Delta_{xx}\Delta_{1x}\Delta_{2x} +\mathcal O(g^2). \end{aligned}\tag{1.20}\] The disconnected free propagator times the vacuum bubble has cancelled. From this, we find \[\begin{aligned} Z[0]=Z_0[0]\Bigg[1 &-\frac{g}{8}\hbar\int d^dx\,\Delta_{xx}^2\\ &+g^2\hbar^2\int d^dx\,d^dy\left( \frac{1}{128}\Delta_{xx}^2\Delta_{yy}^2 +\frac{1}{16}\Delta_{xx}\Delta_{yy}\Delta_{xy}^2 +\frac{1}{48}\Delta_{xy}^4 \right)+\mathcal O(g^3)\Bigg]. \end{aligned}\tag{1.23}\] The three order-$g^2$ vacuum diagrams are

    $\displaystyle _{S=128} \quad+\quad$$\displaystyle _{S=16} \quad+\quad$$\displaystyle _{S=48}.$(1.24)

    As at order $g$, all vacuum-disconnected terms cancel against the normalization by $Z[0]$. The connected result is therefore \[\begin{aligned} G_{12}={}&-\hbar\Delta_{12} +\frac{g}{2}\hbar^2\int d^dx\, \Delta_{1x}\Delta_{xx}\Delta_{x2}\\ &-g^2\hbar^3\int d^dx\,d^dy\Bigg( \frac{1}{4}\Delta_{1x}\Delta_{2x} \Delta_{xy}^2\Delta_{yy} +\frac{1}{4}\Delta_{1x}\Delta_{xx} \Delta_{xy}\Delta_{yy}\Delta_{y2}\\ & +\frac{1}{6}\Delta_{1x}\Delta_{xy}^3\Delta_{y2} \Bigg)+\mathcal O(g^3). \end{aligned}\tag{1.25}\] The order-$g^2$ terms are, in the same order as in the preceding equation,

    $\displaystyle _{S=4} \quad+\quad$$\displaystyle _{S=4} \quad+\quad$$\displaystyle _{S=6}.$(1.26)

2 Generating Functional for One-Particle-Irreducible Diagrams

Define the background field in the presence of $J$ by \[\varphi(x) =\frac{\delta\mathcal W[J]}{\delta J(x)} =\langle\phi(x)\rangle[J],\tag{2.1}\] and introduce the effective action through the Legendre transform \[\Gamma[\varphi] =\int d^4x\,J(x)\varphi(x)-\mathcal W[J].\tag{2.2}\] Recall that the connected generating functional has the expansion \[\mathcal W[J] =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!} \left[ \prod_{k=1}^{n}\int d^4x_k\,J(x_k) \right] G_{\mathrm{CC}}(x_1,\ldots,x_n),\tag{2.3}\] where $G_{\mathrm{CC}}(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ is the fully connected $n$-point function.

  1. Derive the relation \[J(x)=\frac{\delta\Gamma[\varphi]}{\delta\varphi(x)}.\tag{2.4}\]

    Using chain rules, we have \[\begin{aligned} \frac{\delta\Gamma[\varphi]}{\delta\varphi(x)} &= J(x) + \int d^dy\frac{\delta J(y)}{\delta\varphi(x)}\varphi(x) - \int d^dy\frac{\delta \mathcal{W}}{\delta J(y)}\frac{\delta J(y)}{\delta \varphi(x)}=J(x). \end{aligned}\tag{2.5}\]

  2. Consider the Hessian \[\frac{\delta^2\Gamma} {\delta\varphi(x)\delta\varphi(y)}.\tag{2.6}\] Show that its functional inverse is the connected two-point function, \[G_{\mathrm{CC}}(x_1,x_2) =\left[ \frac{\delta^2\Gamma} {\delta\varphi(x_1)\delta\varphi(x_2)} \right]^{-1}, \qquad G_{\mathrm{CC}}(x_1,x_2) =\langle\phi(x_1)\phi(x_2)\rangle_{\mathrm{conn.}}.\tag{2.7}\] You may use the functional chain rule \[\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)} =\int d^4y\, \frac{\delta\varphi(y)}{\delta J(x)} \frac{\delta}{\delta\varphi(y)}.\tag{2.8}\]

    \[\begin{aligned} \frac{\delta^2\Gamma} {\delta\varphi(x_1)\delta\varphi(x_2)} &= \frac{\delta J(x_2)}{\delta \varphi(x_1)} = \left(\frac{\delta^2\mathcal{W}[J]}{\delta J(x_1)\delta J(x_2)}\right)^{-1} \implies G_\mathrm{CC}(x_1,x_2) =\left[ \frac{\delta^2\Gamma} {\delta\varphi(x_1)\delta\varphi(x_2)} \right]^{-1}. \end{aligned}\tag{2.9}\]

  3. Fourier transform the connected two-point function according to \[G_{\mathrm{CC}}(x_1,x_2) =\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\, e^{ik\cdot(x_1-x_2)}\widetilde G(k).\tag{2.10}\] Starting from its perturbative expansion, show that repeated 1PI self-energy insertions form a geometric series and hence give \[\widetilde G(k)=\frac{1}{k^2+m^2+M^2(k)},\tag{2.11}\] where $-M^2(k)$ denotes the sum of one-particle-irreducible insertions. The diagrammatic series is

    Note that at tree-level $\Gamma = S$, and therefore we can write \[\Gamma_2(x,y) = G_0(x,y)^{-1} + \cdots, \quad \Gamma_2(x,y) \equiv \frac{\delta \Gamma[\varphi]}{\delta \varphi(x) \delta\varphi(y)}.\tag{2.12}\] In momentum space, this can be written as \[\Gamma_2(k) = G_0(k)^{-1} +M^2(k) = \tilde{G}(k)^{-1},\tag{2.13}\] where $M^2(k)$ is the 1PI self-energy insertion. Using \[\int d^dz\, \Gamma_2(x,z)G_\mathrm{CC}(z,y) = \delta^d (x-y), \quad \Gamma_2(x,y) \equiv \frac{\delta \Gamma[\varphi]}{\delta \varphi(x) \delta\varphi(y)}.\tag{2.14}\] This gives \[\begin{aligned} \tilde{G}(k) &= \frac{1}{k^2 + m^2 +M^2(k)}\\ &=\frac{1}{k^2+m^2}\sum_{n=0}^\infty\left(\frac{M^2(k)}{k^2+m^2}\right)^n, \end{aligned}\tag{2.15}\] which is exactly the geometric series.
